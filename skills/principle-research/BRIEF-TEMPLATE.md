# Brief template

Write the brief in this structure. Replace the angle-bracket parts, and leave out a section only if it has no entries.

```markdown
# <Topic>: Principles Brief

> Researched: <YYYY-MM-DD> · Depth: <quick | standard | exhaustive> · Mode: <orchestrated | subagents | single agent, self-audited> · <N> sources

## Scope and method

The north-star question. The facets covered. Claims dropped by the audit: <n>. Facets not covered: <list, or "none">. Known gaps.

## Core principles

- **<Principle in one sentence>.** Why it matters and the evidence, with a quote or a specific fact. [1][2] (established)

## Anti-patterns

- **<What reliably makes it bad>.** Same format. [3] (supported)

## Practitioner pain points

- **<What people struggle with>.** How to avoid it. [4] (supported)

## Contested and open questions

- **<Question>.** Position A [5], dated <year>. Position B [6], dated <year>. Which source ranks higher and why. (contested)

## Build checklist

1. <One concrete thing to include, as an instruction: "Show how each figure is counted in a tooltip on its card."> [3][7]
2. <One concrete thing to avoid.> [5]

## Sources

[1] Page title. https://example.com/page-one
[2] Page title. https://example.com/page-two
```

Rules:

- Each claim ends with its source numbers and one confidence label: established, supported, or contested.
- A claim audited weak or unaudited is labelled supported at most.
- Sources are numbered in order of first use, with no gaps, and each URL appears once.
- Every source listed is cited by at least one claim.
- Group long sections under short subheadings by theme, so a reader can skim.
- The build checklist has 8 to 15 items, every item cites a surviving claim, and the items a builder would not think of unprompted come first.
